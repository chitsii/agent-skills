# Structures, a menu

Pick one for the story at hand. None is the default. A structure is a promise about where the turns fall. The architecture sheet decides what the turns are.

| Structure | Shape | Fits | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Three acts | Setup, confrontation, resolution. Turns near the quarter and three-quarter marks. | Plot-driven stories, anything film-shaped. | The tidiest shape and the one models produce unasked. Break one link in the causal chain on purpose. |
| Kishotenketsu (起承転結) | Introduction, development, a turn that recontextualizes, then a joining. The turn is a change of view, not a climax. | Short pieces, quiet stories, anything where the reveal recolors what came before. | The 結 slides into a stated moral. End on an image or an act. |
| Jo-ha-kyu (序破急) | Slow, then breaking, then fast. A pacing shape, not a plot shape. | Action, confrontation, any story that should accelerate. Layer it over another structure. | Acceleration past the interesting part. Let one thread slow down inside the 急. |
| In medias res, then spiral back | Open at the consequence. Go back through causes in whatever order stages the reveals best. | Mysteries, grief stories, anything where withholding the cause raises the stakes. | A shuffled puzzle box. Aim for moderate nonlinearity, 2 to 3 on a 5-point scale. |
| Braided | Two or more threads that alternate and touch late. | Long form, ensemble casts, a subplot that echoes the main plot obliquely. | Both threads resolve on schedule. Let one end early, or not at all. |
| Beat sheet (Save the Cat and relatives) | Fifteen fixed beats at fixed page ratios. | Genre film and its novel equivalents. | The most template-shaped option. Use it only when the genre's readers expect the beats. |

When two fit, prefer the one the genre's readers would not predict from the premise. That choice can be the rarity move.
